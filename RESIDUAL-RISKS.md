# thinkpol-vpn — Residual risks

What the design accepts, on purpose or until a named ticket: the leaks, windows and unprotected cases inside the ground [security.md](docs/design/security.md#threat-model) defends, and last, for completeness, what the threat model leaves undefended. [PRD.md](PRD.md) states its requirements against this table: a requirement holds except for the rows whose **Breaks** column names it; a DESIGN rule named there ("DESIGN rule N"; the rules are in [security.md](docs/design/security.md)) cites its rows the same way.

A change that adds, narrows or removes a residual updates its row here, in the same branch, and the PRD requirement its Breaks column names. Rows are numbered once and never renumbered; a row that no longer holds is struck through with the ticket that closed it.

Columns: **When** says the conditions; **What leaks** says what an observer learns and who (the censor on the path, the domestic resolver, a local app, a node's owner); **Why accepted** gives the reason; **Ticket** is the open ticket that will close or decide it, or, marked "decided", the done ticket whose Notes or findings record the decision to accept it; a decided row whose reason stops holding gets a new ticket; **Mechanism** is the package doc that describes the behaviour.

## IPv6

| # | Residual | When | What leaks, to whom | Breaks | Why accepted | Ticket | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RR10 | No guard without nf_tables | A Linux kernel without nf_tables | IPv6 from interface-bound sockets leaves the physical interface | D6, R2 | Decided not to fail closed: such a device connects, with health `ipv6_guard_unsupported`, rather than not at all | TP-021 (decided) | [`firewall`](internal/dataplane/firewall/doc.go) |
| RR11 | Guard repair windows and what isn't seen | Linux: a change to the guard's table from outside (its flags, a chain, a rule, an element) is repaired within about 1 s; a base chain in *another* table that sets the links' mark before the guard's chain isn't seen at all. macOS: pf changed from outside is repaired within about 10 s; states made meanwhile are killed by the next apply that reloads the anchor successfully, enables pf or sees the bypass verdict change, but a change that comes and goes between two polls is seen by no apply, and its states live on; a load whose read-back the guard refuses stays in force, unverified, while pf is on (never wider than the rules written but for what someone changed in the anchor, rules or table, between the load and the read-back, or a pfctl that optimized the load anyway). Either OS: a failing guard, until a retry (every 5 s) works | Interface-bound IPv6 may leave meanwhile; on Linux for as long as another table marks it; on macOS, for the life of a state made between two polls | D6, R2 | nftables reports changes and pf is polled, and the guard compares its whole table or anchor with what it wrote; a guard failure never fails the routes or a connect; a refused load is kept because flushing it passes all IPv6, and reloading the last verified guard would exempt what the previous Exempt did; another table's chains: setting a mark takes CAP_NET_ADMIN (root), as adding a chain does, and the guard compares the whole value (0x7470), which no other known software sets; the guard can police only its own table without owning the whole ruleset, and a root process that forges the mark has already won | TP-021, TP-078 (decided: the repair windows); TP-095 (decided: a refused load kept); TP-126 (decided: another table's chains) | [`firewall`](internal/dataplane/firewall/doc.go) |
| RR12 | No pf hook, or something past it | macOS: a main ruleset without `anchor "com.apple/*"` (replaced, or not loaded yet at boot); a `pass quick` above the hook, an interface pf skips (`set skip on`, but `lo0` and the utun) or an anchor under `com.apple` that sorts before the guard's; a `pass quick` inside an anchor the main ruleset evaluates before the hook | Interface-bound IPv6 unconfined for the connection, or while the bypass lasts; health says unsupported (no hook: the guard stops for good), not enforced (a quick pass above the hook, a skipped interface, an earlier `com.apple` anchor: the anchor stays in place for the rest, checked within about 10 s), or nothing (a quick pass inside an earlier anchor of the main ruleset, which isn't seen) | D6, R2 | As RR10, and `/etc/pf.conf` is never edited: the guard reports other software's pf settings as not enforced and doesn't undo them; a quick pass inside an earlier main-ruleset anchor isn't read until TP-129 | TP-046 (boot race); TP-129 (a quick pass inside an earlier main-ruleset anchor); TP-126 (decided: reported, not undone) | [`firewall`](internal/dataplane/firewall/doc.go) |
| RR13 | What pf can't express | macOS | MLD-typed ICMPv6 to any multicast group with any hop limit (root only); neighbour solicitations to a DNS upstream's address may be forwarded; DNS to each direct upstream's address and port passes for any socket | D6 | pf has no hop-limit match and no socket mark | TP-078 (decided) | [`firewall`](internal/dataplane/firewall/doc.go) |

| RR15 | No IPv6 proof on macOS | macOS with native IPv6 | Unproven that the guard blocks its leak paths | D6 | Evidence gap: needs a network with native IPv6 | TP-096 | [`firewall`](internal/dataplane/firewall/doc.go) |
| RR16 | Another VPN after a crash | macOS: another VPN holds `::/1`, and the agent died | Our crash-time IPv6 reject routes are missing. |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RR17 | No kill switch | The tunnel is down, any OS | Traffic and DNS go direct | D4, R4, R2 | It is M2's: holding traffic while the tunnel is down needs the allow-LAN and pause options built with it, or it strands members behind captive portals | TP-048; Windows TP-076 | [`firewall`](internal/dataplane/firewall/doc.go) |
| RR18 | IPv4 after a crash | Linux and macOS, the agent died connected | All IPv4 in the clear until the next start; on Linux with systemd-resolved the TUN's link goes with the TUN, so names go to the uplink's resolvers too. IPv6 stays blocked | D4, R4, R2 | Fail-closed for IPv4 needs the kill switch's firewall, which isn't built | TP-048, TP-046 | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR19 | IPv4 when the TUN drops | Linux, connected; macOS unverified (its `/1` routes become rejects only after the same debounce) | IPv4 follows the physical default route for at least about 200 ms (a burst of events restarts the debounce; a failing re-apply stretches it to the 5 s retries) until the unreachable default is in. Unverified: with systemd-resolved, if a down TUN's `~.` stops counting the way a gone one's does (RR18), names go to the uplink's resolvers for as long as the TUN is down | D4, R2 | An unreachable default in place earlier would answer the kernel's gateway lookups for interfaces the rules don't know yet | TP-057 (decided); TP-048 | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR20 | A device removed or retrying | Its map removed it, or a start failed and is being retried | Its data plane, routes and guard are down and the OS's DNS is back, so its traffic and names go direct, IPv6 included | D4, D6, R4, R2 | Without a kill switch there is nothing to hold traffic while no data plane runs | TP-048 | [`agent`](internal/agent/doc.go) |
| RR21 | Interface-bound IPv4 | Linux: `SO_BINDTODEVICE`, `IP_UNICAST_IF`, `IP_PKTINFO`; macOS: `IP_BOUND_IF`, sockets routed by their source address | They leave by the physical interface (`ping -I`, connectivity checks) | R2 | Routing can't hold them, and the leak guard is IPv6 only | TP-048 | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR22 | Foreign routes on macOS | A new on-link subnet or classless route (TunnelVision, LocalNet), until the watcher re-applies (a debounce); a foreign host route over a tunnelled destination, always | Tunnelled destinations go out the physical interface (IPv6 to them is the guard's) | R2 | macOS has no policy routing; a host route can't be outranked, only counted (`policy_rules_ahead`) | TP-019 (decided); TP-048 | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR23 | A foreign host route to a peer | macOS: a host route (a DHCP classless route among them) to a peer's overlay address | Device-to-device packets to that peer leave the physical interface in the clear, not through WireGuard; health `overlay_routes_ahead` | DESIGN rule 1 | As RR22: nothing outranks a host route; blocking it is a firewall's | TP-048 | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR24 | Policy rules ahead of ours | Linux: other software's rules before the tunnel's | They may route around the tunnel | R2 | Reported (`policy_rules_ahead`); taking priority over other software's rules would break it | TP-018 (decided) | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR25 | Endpoint throws | Any OS | Any app's traffic to an entry's address goes direct; behind a CDN that would be every site on the address | R2, D6 | A throw is per address; narrowing it to the transport's flow waits for a transport behind a CDN | TP-018 (decided) | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR26 | Scoped route to a DNS upstream | macOS, a direct upstream off the link | Any socket bound to the physical interface reaches that address around the tunnel | R2 | An unscoped route would take every app's traffic to it around the tunnel | TP-019 (decided) | [`routetable`](internal/dataplane/routetable/doc.go) |
| RR27 | Local ranges always direct | Any preset | LANs, link-local and multicast bypass the tunnel; hosts on a public on-link LAN are tunnelled instead | — | A LAN is the member's own network; a public LAN opt-in can come later | TP-015, TP-057 (decided) | [`rules`](internal/dataplane/rules/doc.go) |
| RR28 | No rules, or a reader too old | A section without rules, or rules its node can't read | Nothing outside the overlay goes through the tunnel, and names go to the domestic resolver | R4, R2, D6 | Compatibility with M0's maps, and a node can't enforce what it can't read | TP-015 (decided) | [`rules`](internal/dataplane/rules/doc.go) |
| RR29 | A name rule undercut by an address rule | Route-table mode: `domain → chain`, and a later address rule sends the answer's addresses direct | The name resolves through the tunnel, but the flow (its SNI included) goes around it | R8 | Domain rules are enforced by DNS only until userspace mode; reported in status | TP-015 (decided) | [`rules`](internal/dataplane/rules/doc.go) |

## Transports and probing

| # | Residual | When | What leaks, to whom | Breaks | Why accepted | Ticket | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RR30 | `udp` is the only transport | Until `reality` | Timing (rekeys, keepalives), sizes (no padding by default), a constant 8-byte connection id per hour and the 5-tuple link a node's flows; a probe meets a silent port, since no camouflage exists and unauthenticated datagrams are dropped | C2, C6 | It is the baseline the lab must catch; the disguised transport is M2's | TP-087 | [`udp`](internal/dataplane/transport/udp/doc.go) |
| RR31 | Once camouflage exists: server-side tells | `reality` and `tls` | The server's own hello differs from the camouflage site's; every failed probe makes the entry dial the target, which sees each probe | C2 | Removing them needs the target's TLS stack; the lab measures them | TP-026, TP-032 | [`transport`](internal/dataplane/transport/doc.go) |
| RR32 | A fleet-wide camouflage target | Until targets are chosen per node | One target shared by the fleet fingerprints it | C2 | The target is picked at provisioning, which isn't built | TP-038 | [`transport`](internal/dataplane/transport/doc.go) |
| RR33 | A datagram authenticates the link, not which end sent it | `udp`: whoever holds a link's secret: its two ends, whoever built the map, whoever opens either end's section; once TP-063 lands, the gatekeeper | It can seal datagrams the listener accepts as the dialer's; WireGuard still authenticates the peer | — | WireGuard authenticates every peer, so forged datagrams can't put data into the tunnel; what a holder can do happens before it: take its link's reply address (it stays until 30 s of silence from the forged sender, since a later creation time outranks the real dialer's), get probe answers, have frames relayed down a route under a source id the node knows, and spend the shared listener's decryption, which happens before any per-space accounting (RR38). A signature per datagram would add a second public-key layer, an estimated 15–30× the seal's CPU and 64 bytes less MTU on every datagram, and still not stop a stolen device, which holds the key | TP-125 (decided) | [`udp`](internal/dataplane/transport/udp/doc.go) |
