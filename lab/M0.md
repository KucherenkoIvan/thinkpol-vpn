# M0 acceptance

M0's "done when" ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#milestones)) as lab tests, in [`m0_test.go`](m0_test.go). They run with the rest of the lab:

```sh
make lab-test                                                     # everything
go test -tags lab -count=1 -v -run 'TestM0|TestCaptureFlagsRawWireGuard' ./lab/   # just these (needs Docker)
```

## Setup

The standard topology (`device-a`, `device-b` ── router ── `server-1`). As a space's admin would, the `internet` node makes the keys and the map with `thinkpol dev keygen` and `thinkpol dev map build` from [`maps/m0.yaml`](maps/m0.yaml): device-a and device-b each have a path to server-1, and a path to each other only through server-1. Each node gets its own key, `space.pub` and the map in `/var/lib/thinkpol`, and runs `thinkpol up`; server-1 runs as a supervisor with one worker for the space (the helpers are shared with `TestUpRunsTheM0Space` in [`up_test.go`](up_test.go)). Overlay addresses: server-1 `100.64.0.1` and `<ula>::1`, device-a `.2`/`::2`, device-b `.3`/`::3`.

`TestM0` runs twice, each in its own lab: **underlay=ipv4** with `m0.yaml` as is (server-1's IPv4 endpoint comes first, so the devices dial it over IPv4), and **underlay=ipv6** with `m0.yaml` minus the IPv4 endpoint, so everything crosses the router over IPv6. Before anything starts, the test reads device-a's opened section (`dev map inspect`) to confirm what it relies on: one link, to server-1, at the underlay under test, and server-1 as device-b's only first hop.

## What it proves

Every capture is checked complete before its packets are trusted (`Capture.Complete`: tcpdump dropped nothing and cut nothing short), so "absent from the capture" means absent from the wire.

| Subtest | Checks |
|---|---|
| `server-1 runs the space in its own worker` | exactly one `thinkpol worker`, a child of `thinkpol up`, in its own network, user, PID and mount namespaces, with no root among its real, effective, saved and filesystem uids; server-1's overlay addresses are in the worker's network namespace and not in the node's |
| `direct: devices and server-1` | device-a and device-b ping server-1 over overlay IPv4 and IPv6, and server-1 (from its worker's namespace) pings both devices over both. For a burst of 20 large (1200-byte) pings from device-a, server-1's capture holds at least as many large datagrams in from device-a and out to it as device-a got replies (all 20; a burst that loses a reply is tried once more), and none to or from device-b |
| `relayed: device to device through server-1` | device-a and device-b ping each other over overlay IPv4 and IPv6, both ways. For a 20-ping burst from device-a to device-b, server-1's capture holds, for each reply device-a got, a large datagram in from device-a and out to device-b, and one in from device-b and out to device-a: the relay is server-1 |
| `no traffic between the devices but through server-1` | the devices share a network, so direct traffic wouldn't cross the router; each device's own capture of the whole run holds no packet to or from the other's addresses (link-local included), multicast ICMPv6 (neighbour discovery, router solicitations, MLD) and IGMP aside, and every UDP datagram the device sent, multicast included, went to server-1's listener |
| `no WireGuard on the wire at the router (C1)` | no UDP payload that crossed the router over the whole run, from before the first handshake, starts with a WireGuard header (type 1–4 and three zero bytes, the loosest part of the signature) or ends in a whole WireGuard message behind some other header (`lab.WireGuardOffset`); the data plane's datagrams are all over the underlay under test, and in the IPv6-only run nothing crosses over IPv4 at all. Not vacuous: the first data-plane datagram is a device dialing server-1, and at least the bursts' 480 sightings are there (the router sees each datagram twice; 2 direct bursts cross once each way, 2 relayed bursts twice) |

The router's figures, for scale: 678–690 data-plane datagram sightings per run (686 in most), 480 of them exactly the bursts every time, the rest the single pings, handshakes, keepalives and path probes, whose count depends on timers.

`TestCaptureFlagsRawWireGuard` keeps the C1 check honest: plain kernel WireGuard between device-a and server-1, captured the same way at the router, is flagged by `lab.LooksLikeWireGuard` (type, zero reserved bytes, 148-byte initiation, 92-byte response, transport data in 16-byte steps) for every datagram, initiation, response and data alike. `TestLooksLikeWireGuard` covers the signature's edges in `make test`.

Each check was seen to fail when broken: a datagram sent straight from device-a to device-b, or a multicast UDP datagram from device-b, fails the no-direct subtest; a WireGuard-shaped datagram across the router fails C1, as does an initiation behind 18 other bytes, and any IPv4 UDP in the IPv6-only run.

## Covered elsewhere

- **Need to know** (S1, the map's part of M0): each node's opened section holds only its peers, neighbours and relayed destinations, and each link secret is in exactly its two ends' sections (`TestSectionsHoldOnlyWhatTheirNodeNeeds`, `internal/devmap`); a node opens no section but its own (`TestOpenOnlyOwnSection`, `internal/netmap`); the lab's own map sequence, each node's section checked (`TestDevMapLabWorkflow`, `cmd/thinkpol`). `TestUpRunsTheM0Space` checks that each node's state directory holds only its own key, `space.pub` and the map, and that `thinkpol up` leaves nothing behind.
- **Map changes** (by file and by SIGHUP) and clean shutdown: `TestUpRunsTheM0Space`.
- **udp on the wire** without a lab: `TestCaptureShowsNoWireGuard` (`internal/dataplane/transport/udp`) also checks that no WireGuard message's first 16 bytes appear anywhere in the datagrams.

## Not in M0

Direct device ↔ device paths: devices have no endpoints, so device ↔ device traffic always goes through a server (direct paths come after the MVP, DESIGN.md "Milestones"). "Directly" in M0 is device ↔ server. The censor (blocklists, DPI, prober) comes with TP-024; this capture only shows what a censor at the router would see.
