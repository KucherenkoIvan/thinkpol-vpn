## Goal
The node's public address belongs to the supervisor, which authenticates every connection and hands it to the right space's worker.

## Scope
- Supervisor listens on the configured public ports of each transport.
- Runs the transport's authentication, maps the credential to a space, hands the connection over: stream connections by passing the file descriptor (`SCM_RIGHTS`), datagram flows by forwarding per connection id over the worker socket.
- Unauthenticated connections go to the camouflage handler and never reach a worker.
- Per-space credential tables loaded from each space's map.

## Acceptance
- [x] in the lab, two spaces on one server (hand-made maps): each worker receives only its own links. `supervisor.TestTwoSpacesShareAListener` (lab node, real sandboxed workers): space A's map section lists two udp dialers, B's one; their inbound tables come from `dataplane.InboundFrom` of the hand-written sections. Each worker accepts exactly its own dialers' links (identity `<its space>/<node>`), reads their packets, and its replies reach the dialers through the public socket; `WorkerInfo.Shared` shows Forwarded = Replies = its own dialer count. In-process: `TestWorkersGetOnlyTheirOwnLinks` (also: an entry claiming another space is stamped with the space whose map listed it), `TestASecretTwoSpacesShareIsRefused`, and udp's conformance suite run through the supervisor's shared core (`TestConformanceBehindTheSupervisor`).
- [x] bad credentials never reach any worker. Lab: an unknown secret, a one-byte-off copy of a real secret and random junk of four sizes are all counted `Unauthenticated` on the listener (6 of 6), and each worker's own udp listener reports `unauthenticated=0` (nothing it couldn't authenticate was ever forwarded to it). In-process: `TestBadCredentialsNeverReachAWorker` flips every byte of a genuine datagram in turn (all go to the camouflage, none to the worker; the genuine one then goes through); `TestStreamsAreHandedOver` sends a probe without credentials whole to the camouflage, with the consumed bytes.
- [x] (from TP-009's notes) ≥ 50 Mbit/s through the shared listener on a lab node pinned to one CPU. `TestSharedListenerThroughput`: supervisor and both workers pinned to CPU 0 (`taskset`, every thread checked in `/proc`), the load on the other CPUs: **≈ 980 Mbit/s one way** into a worker, **≈ 480 Mbit/s each way** echoed through it, 1400-byte packets, two runs (976.6/480.8 and 988.8/477.2). See Notes for what that does and doesn't say.

## Notes
- **Decided in the TP-005 review (2026-10-10):** the tls auth token rides in the ClientHello (e.g. an HMAC of the credential and a coarse timestamp in the legacy session id, via uTLS). The supervisor peeks at the ClientHello without terminating TLS and keeps a replay cache. Authenticated: it hands the fd plus the consumed bytes to the space's worker, which terminates TLS with its self-signed, pinned certificate. Anything else is proxied whole to the camouflage target, so probes see the real site's certificate. See DESIGN.md "Server agent: one worker per space".
- **Port seams (TP-005):** `transport.Routable.Router(peers)` gives a `transport.Router`. Use `RouteDatagram(d)` to authenticate a datagram (tag and timestamp) and get its `Identity`, then forward it untouched over the worker socket. On the worker side, `udp.Transport.ListenOn(pc, ListenConfig{Peers, Camouflage})` serves a `net.PacketConn` fed by the supervisor. `RouteStream(conn)` and `transport.HandoffListener.ServeStream(conn, consumed)` are the stream side, implemented here and in TP-028. `udp/router_test.go` is a working in-process sketch.
- When merging the spaces' tables into one `Peers`, stamp `Identity.Space` from the map it came from, never from the map's contents. Charge a space's fair-share bucket after its worker accepts a datagram. A credential shared by two entries is dropped for both and reported (`Router.Rejected`).
- **Replays and fair share (TP-005 delta review):** `Router.RouteDatagram` keeps no replay state, so it forwards replays of the last five minutes to the worker, which drops them. Those datagrams cost the worker a decryption, but charging fair share after the worker accepts means nothing charges for them. If that matters under load, give the supervisor a small replay filter (per credential, a window of recently forwarded nonces or counters) in front of the worker socket.

- **Done in TP-010 (2026-10-11):**
  - **Shape.** `supervisor.SetListeners([]Listener{Transport, Address, Settings, Camouflage})` opens the node's public listeners (from the node owner's map; guests never open ports) for every space; `Listeners() []ListenerInfo{ID, Transport, Address, Routed, Unauthenticated, Rejected}`. `Space.Inbound map[transport][]PeerLink` is each space's own inbound table (`dataplane.InboundFrom` of its section); the supervisor merges them per transport into one `transport.PeerTable` and stamps every entry's `Space` with the space it was listed under, whatever the entry said. A secret listed twice (in one space or across two) is dropped for both and shows in `ListenerInfo.Rejected` (udp's `indexPeers`). `Options.Transports` (a `transport.Registry`) and `Options.FairShare`. `WorkerInfo.Shared SharedCounters{Forwarded, ForwardedBytes, Streams, NoWorker, WorkerFull, OverShare, Replies, ReplyBytes, RepliesRefused}`. Core in `supervisor/shared.go` (no build tag, unit-tested on any OS with in-process workers); Linux wiring in `supervisor_linux.go`.
  - **Transport port.** `transport.PacketRoutable{Routable; ListenPacket(ctx, ListenConfig) (net.PacketConn, error); ListenForwarded(pc, ListenConfig) Listener}` and `transport.StreamRoutable{Routable; ListenStream(ctx, ListenConfig) (net.Listener, error); Handoff(ListenConfig) (HandoffListener, error)}`. udp implements `PacketRoutable` (`Listen` = `ListenPacket` + `ListenOn`); a transport that is neither can't sit behind the shared listener (SetListeners reports it).
  - **Routing.** One goroutine per public datagram socket: read (`ReadFromUDPAddrPort`, no allocation) → `Router.RouteDatagram` → the space's route (an atomic snapshot of space → route; a route holds the current incarnation's sockets in an atomic pointer, set after Ready and cleared when the incarnation ends, so a restarting space drops with `NoWorker`) → `FairShare.Allow` → `TrySendDatagram` on the data socket (never blocks; `ErrFull` → `WorkerFull`) → charge. Routes are published before the credential tables, so a link that authenticates always has a route; a space that leaves is unreachable before its worker stops. Unauthenticated (or authenticated for no served space) → the listener's camouflage (`Drop` by default: udp answers nothing). Streams: one goroutine per accepted connection, `RouteStream` under a 10 s read deadline (cut short when the listener closes), then the descriptor (`File()`) + consumed bytes go by `TrySend` on the worker's control socket, or the connection whole to the camouflage.
  - **Replies only to authenticated remotes.** One goroutine per worker incarnation reads its data socket and sends each reply out of the listener it names, only if that (listener, remote) pair was the source of one of the space's own forwarded datagrams in the last 5 minutes (≤ 16384 remembered per space, expired ones swept when full); otherwise `RepliesRefused`. So a guest worker can't use the node's public address to reach anyone else, whatever egress its space has (`TestWorkersOnlyAnswer`). Written into DESIGN.md. A malformed message on the data socket ends the incarnation, like an unknown control kind.
  - **IPC (version 2).** Data socket, both ways: `Kind 5 (Datagram) | listener id [4, BE] | family [1: 4 or 6] | address [16, IPv4 in the first 4] | port [2, BE] | datagram` (`DatagramHeaderSize` 23, `MaxDatagramSize` = 64 KiB − 24), sent with `sendmsg` over two iovecs (no copy): `TrySendDatagram` from the supervisor, blocking `SendDatagram` from the worker (backpressure stays in that worker). Control socket: `Kind 7 (Listeners)`, JSON `[]ipc.Listener{ID, Transport, Stream, Address}`, after Ready and on every change (ids are never reused within a supervisor's life, so a worker keeps an entry across messages); `Kind 6 (Stream)`, `listener id [4, BE] | consumed bytes`, with the connection's descriptor attached (`ipc.StreamMessage`/`DecodeStream`). `ipc.ErrFull` moved to the platform-independent file.
  - **Worker side.** `Session.Listeners <-chan []worker.SharedListener` (latest-wins): `{ipc.Listener; Packets net.PacketConn (datagram); Streams <-chan worker.Stream{Conn, Consumed} (stream); Done}`. A datagram listener's `Packets` queues up to 1024 forwarded datagrams (then drops, counted), `WriteTo` sends a reply; `Close` is final (serve each listener with one transport listener while it is listed); deadlines are refused (udp doesn't use them). A stream handed to a datagram or unknown listener, or with a full queue (64), is closed.
  - **Fair share (hook only).** `supervisor.FairShare{Allow(space, n) bool; Charge(space, n)}`: asked before forwarding a datagram or sending a reply, charged after the worker's socket accepted it or after it went out. Nothing is charged for unauthenticated traffic, a full socket or a refusal (`TestFairShareChargedAfterTheWorkerAccepts`). Replays inside udp's five-minute window are routed (the Router keeps no replay state) and so charged to the space whose credential they carry; only an on-path attacker can replay, and it could drop that traffic anyway. A supervisor replay filter stays an option for TP-040 if that matters.
  - **No head-of-line blocking.** A stuck worker (full data socket) loses its own datagrams only; the other space's flow on the same socket goes on (`TestAStuckWorkerStallsOnlyItsSpace`). Stream routing is per connection; handoffs never block.
  - **Performance.** `TestSharedListenerThroughput` (lab node): the supervisor and both workers (one busy, one idle) pinned to CPU 0, one udp dialer on the other CPUs offering 1400-byte packets as fast as it can. Measured at the worker's link: ≈ 980 Mbit/s one way (≈ 88 k packets/s); echo, both directions at once through the one CPU: ≈ 480 Mbit/s each way. Under that overload the data socket drops (`WorkerFull` ≈ 0.5 M in a run), as designed. No batching (recvmmsg/sendmmsg) was needed for the target, so none was added; that and SO_REUSEPORT fast paths remain options. Caveats: the lab node's CPU is a fast desktop core, not a VPS vCPU, so read the margin (~10× one way) rather than the absolute number; the path measured is public socket → supervisor (one AEAD open) → data socket → worker (a second open, replay filter) → link, without WireGuard, the TUN or a second hop, which the PRD's end-to-end ≥ 50 Mbit/s also includes (TP-012/TP-033 measure that). The lab can't run `-race` (no cgo in nodes); the race detector ran on the host over the in-process tests.
  - **Not done here, with reason.** No lab test drives a stream handoff through real sandboxed workers: there is no stream transport until TP-028. The seam is tested in-process (`TestStreamsAreHandedOver`: a line-based fake transport, a real TCP connection, its descriptor duplicated as SCM_RIGHTS does), and descriptor passing over the real socket is TP-009's `TestPassesFileDescriptors`; the worker's `KindStream` handling (`net.FileConn`) runs only once TP-028 has a stream transport in the lab.
- **For TP-011 (`thinkpol up`):** in supervisor mode build `supervisor.Options{Transports: registry, …}`; per served space, verify and open its map, `Space{ID, Config, Inbound: dataplane.InboundFrom(section, reg, transport.SpaceID(id)).Links}`; `SetListeners` from the **node owner's** section `Node.Listeners` only (dual-stack: both `0.0.0.0:p` and `[::]:p`). Re-run both on every map change. In the worker, `agent.RunDataPlane` serves `Session.Listeners` instead of opening sockets: for each datagram listener, `reg.Get(l.Transport).(transport.PacketRoutable).ListenForwarded(l.Packets, ListenConfig{Peers: engine.Inbound(l.Transport)})` and `go engine.Serve(l.Transport, listener)`; stop serving when `l.Done` closes. Workers' veths carry only their dialed links and egress.
- **For TP-013 (ingress off):** filter `Space.Inbound` before passing it in: with ingress off, drop entries whose `netmap.InboundLink.PeerKind` is a device (they become unauthenticated, i.e. camouflage). `PeerLink` has no kind, so this happens where the section is converted. The worker's own table should match (`engine.Inbound`).
- **For TP-028 (tls):** implement `transport.StreamRoutable`: `ListenStream` opens the public TCP listener, `Router(peers).RouteStream` peeks the ClientHello and must return **every** byte it read (they go to the worker or to the camouflage, which proxies them first), `Handoff(cfg)` is the worker's `HandoffListener`; feed it with `for s := range l.Streams { hl.ServeStream(s.Conn, s.Consumed) }` until `l.Done`. The supervisor's read deadline for routing is 10 s. The camouflage proxy is the listener's `Camouflage.Stream` and must not block (start its own goroutine). Add the lab test of a handoff through real workers that TP-010 couldn't.
- **For TP-040 (caps):** implement `supervisor.FairShare` (per-space token buckets; `Allow` must be cheap and non-blocking, it runs per datagram on the listener's goroutine). It sees both directions of datagram traffic. Handed-over streams bypass it: cap them on the socket before the handoff (e.g. `SO_MAX_PACING_RATE` on the passed descriptor) or on the uplink, plus TP-009's veth/cgroup limits. Guests' `Inbound` come from the guests' own maps; only the owner's listeners are opened.

## Review findings
Two reviewers (2026-10-11); the routing core held up and no cross-space leak was found. Decided by the orchestrator.

Blocker:
- [ ] B1 Reply-table DoS via replays. (a) A supervisor-side replay filter now: per credential, a sliding window on udp's sender and counter, in front of forwarding and of remembering a remote, so replays are dropped before they cost the space anything. (b) Reply peers kept per link identity with a small cap (8 remotes per link, evicting that link's oldest), so nothing one link does displaces another's. (c) Amortized expiry (a sweep at most once per interval). (d) The reply window refreshed only by live (non-replayed) inbound datagrams and shortened to ~2 minutes, with the reason documented. (e) Tests: a replay adds no entry; a flood of new sources on one space doesn't stall another space's flow (measured); full-table behaviour counted.

Should-fix:
- [ ] 2 Control sends with a deadline: ipc `SendTimeout` for Listeners, Config and Stop (StopTimeout); on timeout the worker is killed and restarted with backoff. Test: a worker that stops reading fd 3 doesn't hang SetSpaces or Close.
- [ ] 3 Precise DESIGN wording for the reply rule (only to source addresses its authenticated, non-replayed datagrams came from in the last ~2 minutes) and the ticket's replay note (replays need only a passive tap).
- [ ] 4 Stream routing: at most 1024 connections in flight per listener; on overload the connection goes to the camouflage; counted.
- [ ] 5 One source of truth for inbound tables: `dataplane.AcceptedInbound(section, reg, space, linkMTU)`, used by `Engine.Apply` and the supervisor's composition; TP-013's ingress filter goes inside it later. Fix the TP-011/TP-013 notes.
- [ ] 6 Counters: NoSpace apart from Unauthenticated; WorkerFull only for ErrFull, otherwise NoWorker; StreamsRefused; RepliesRefused split (NotAllowed, NoListener, OverShare); ReplyErrors; `RouteDatagram` returns a verdict (Unknown, Routed, Stale, Replay) counted per space; worker-side drops (QueueFull, UnknownListener) exposed through the Session. Telemetry: the supervisor periodically sends each space's SharedCounters to its worker (a new IPC kind, IPC v3) so the worker seals them into its own telemetry (TP-041 note); owner-only fields (ListenerInfo.Address, Rejected) never go to telemetry, and the docs say so.
- [ ] 7 Honest signatures: `ListenPacket` returns `*net.UDPConn`; `ListenForwarded` returns `(Listener, error)`; `Rejected()` on `Router`; no generic ReadFrom slow path.
- [ ] 8 `SharedListener.Listen(reg, cfg) (transport.Listener, error)`, so TP-011's RunDataPlane takes one Inbound abstraction in device and worker modes; the worker's public API copies fields instead of embedding `ipc.Listener`.
- [ ] 9 Throughput: a paced run at 100 Mbit/s (1 ms bursts) with loss < 0.5% by the supervisor's counters; SO_SNDBUF/SO_RCVBUF of 1–4 MiB on the data socket pair; Notes: over loopback the receive softirq runs on the sender's CPU.
- [ ] 10 DESIGN: a pacing cap on a handed-over stream limits only its sends; uploads need policing (tc ingress/IFB) or a read-rate limit. Note for TP-040.
- [ ] 11 TP-014 Notes (decided): a worker's outgoing dials to endpoints listed in its own section are allowed through the root namespace (masqueraded) whatever the egress setting; egress off still blocks everything else (M3 entry→exit needs it).
- [ ] 12 Each "For TP-0xx" note copied into that ticket's Notes (TP-011, TP-013, TP-014, TP-028, TP-040, TP-041), with a Log line each.

Nits:
- [ ] rollback re-syncs the shared tables; SetListeners documents that a settings change reopens the socket (the port can be lost if the reopen fails); `reply()` unmaps IPv4-mapped destinations and keeps zones; TestWorkersOnlyAnswer without its race and not vacuous; expiry and full-table tests; `bytes.Compare` inline.
